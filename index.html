Human minds. Artificial agents. Shared worlds.

Intelligence
in interaction.

I study how cooperation emerges, is maintained and breaks-down among humans, among artificial agents, and between humans and AI.

My work connects computational cognitive science and artificial intelligence, combining behavioural experiments, computational models, and simulations of interacting agents.

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Machine Learning Human Behavior lab at Heidelberg University's Interdisciplinary Center for Scientific Computing (IWR). My current work examines when communication helps AI agents learn to cooperate and coordinate.

During my PhD at UCL, I studied how people learn about opponents and transfer that knowledge across games. I also developed computer partners based on human behaviour to test interventions aimed at restoring cooperation.
